THE CICAN FOUNDATION

24 PRINCIPLES.
One responsibility.

These principles guide how CICAN approaches documentation, creativity, historical evidence, public participation and long-term preservation.

01 — 04

Identity & experience

Recognise the value of every story and make history something people can experience.

01

A name deserves to be remembered.

A business represents years of work, decisions, relationships and human lives. Its name deserves the opportunity to remain part of history.

02

Every business deserves equal respect.

Historical worth is not measured by size, wealth, popularity or influence. Every business has a story.

03

History should be experienced.

Photography, original line-art and printed colouring books can invite people to engage with a place and its story in a tangible way.

04

Preservation requires participation.

People help preserve history by sharing photographs, documents, knowledge and memories—and by asking questions.

05 — 08

People, continuity & truth

Keep human contributions visible, build for continuity and never invent the past.

05

The business is the subject. Humanity is the story.

Founders, employees, suppliers, customers, families and neighbours all contribute to a business's history.

06

An archive should outlive its original moment.

Records should remain understandable and accessible as businesses, technologies and generations change.

07

Never depend on a single place to remember.

Our long-term ambition is to combine digital and physical preservation, independent copies and appropriate safeguards.

08

Preserve the story. Never invent the history.

Verified facts, personal recollections, interpretations and unknown information must remain clearly distinguishable.

09 — 12

Identity of the record

Give every subject a lasting identity and keep the history of the record transparent.

09

Every archive has a permanent identity.

Each business archive receives a unique CICAN ID connecting its evidence, locations, publications and future contributions.

10

Every detail has an identity.

Individual locations, buildings and significant objects can receive their own unique identifiers.

11

Nothing loses its historical identity.

Relocation, demolition, closure or a change of ownership does not erase an item's original identity.

12

History must remain transparent.

Additions, corrections and revisions should be documented so earlier information remains traceable.

13 — 16

Sources, place & connection

Connect historical claims to their sources, geography and wider relationships.

13

Every source deserves recognition.

Record who contributed material, where it came from, when it was received and what permissions apply.

14

History belongs to a place.

Record geographical information wherever it can be established, distinguishing verified coordinates from estimates.

15

One place. Many chapters.

Document how a location's buildings, occupants, names, uses and surroundings change through time.

16

No story exists in isolation.

Connect people, businesses, buildings, objects and places while keeping each record independently identifiable.

17 — 20

Discovery, access & learning

Keep unanswered questions open and invite people to discover, contribute and learn.

17

The unknown must also be preserved.

Incomplete records can still hold value. Preserve what is known, identify what is uncertain and leave room for discovery.

18

History belongs to everyone.

Invite public contributions, then review their sources, permissions and verification status before presenting claims as established history.

19

Open knowledge. Sustainable preservation.

Make shareable historical knowledge accessible while sustaining the work through creative and commissioned services.

20

Preserve history. Educate the future.

Help future generations investigate local history, architecture, geography, family heritage and the evidence behind a story.

21 — 24

Integrity & responsibility

Protect the dignity of people, the integrity of evidence and the interests of future generations.

21

Every human story matters.

Ordinary people's contributions deserve recognition, with consent, privacy, dignity and evidence respected.

22

Independence and impartiality.

Document evidence fairly, regardless of a subject's wealth, reputation, popularity, success or controversy.

23

Protect historical evidence.

Do not manipulate or conceal records to suit the present. Handle lawful restrictions and privacy responsibly and transparently.

24

Responsibility to future generations.

Ask whether someone centuries from now can understand what was preserved, where it came from and why it mattered.

OUR LONG-TERM TEST

If someone discovers this archive 500 years from now, will they understand?

What we preserved. Where the information came from. What is known, what remains uncertain, and why it mattered.
